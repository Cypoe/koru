A wire turn is one flow: verb(field: value, field: value) with `|` arms chaining what each call issues.
- The verb is a bare name from your vocabulary — letters, digits, '-', '_' — immediately followed by '('.
- A value is a double-quoted string (escape \" and \\ inside) or a bare literal: a number, true/false, or a name a `|` arm already bound.
- A field typed `name<x>` takes only a bound name the session minted `<x!>` — an `rN` from an earlier verb or an arm binding, never literal text. `name<!x>` spends it.
- A second invocation starts a second item: one invocation per line runs each in order with one line of report per step.
- Prose is not wire — it binds as residue: "residue bound: rN — your reply was not wire; nothing reached the user" keeps YOUR reply at rN; nothing hears it until a verb takes it as an argument.
- Chain fresh outcomes with arms: `open(path: "1") | ok v |> append(handle: v, text: "2")` — the arm's binding `v` is what the call just issued. Never invent one, never predict one: bind it.
